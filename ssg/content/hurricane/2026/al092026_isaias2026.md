## Official Advisory Discussion
At 400 AM CDT (0900 UTC), the eye of Hurricane Isaias was located
near latitude 26.4 North, longitude 88.0 West. Isaias is moving
toward the north-northeast near 15 mph (24 km/h). A turn toward the
north is expected later today. On the forecast track, Isaias is
expected to approach the U.S. northern Gulf Coast this afternoon and
make landfall within the warning area tonight or early Saturday.
 
Air Force and NOAA Hurricane Hunter data indicate that the maximum
sustained winds have increased to near 110 mph (175 km/h) with
higher gusts. Isaias is likely to become a major hurricane this
morning. Slight weakening is expected before landfall, but it is
expected to remain a dangerous hurricane.
 
Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 30 miles (45 km) from the
center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 185 miles
(295 km).
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 963 mb (28.44 inches) 
based on Air Force dropsonde data.
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Isaias can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header MIATCDAT4 and WMO header WTNT44 KNHC.
 
STORM SURGE: The combination of a dangerous storm surge and the
tide will cause normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by
rising waters moving inland from the shoreline. The water could
reach the following heights above ground somewhere in the indicated
areas if the peak surge occurs at the time of high tide...
 
Dauphin Island, AL to Indian Pass, FL...5-7 ft
Indian Pass, FL to Steinhatchee River, FL...4-6 ft
Steinhatchee River, FL to Suwannee River, FL...3-5 ft
Mouth of the Mississippi River, LA to Dauphin Island, AL...3-5 ft
Mobile Bay...3-5 ft
Suwannee River, FL to Yankeetown, FL...2-4 ft
Yankeetown, FL to Egmont Key, FL...1-3 ft
Lake Pontchartrain...1-3 ft
Morgan City, LA to Mouth of the Mississippi River, LA...1-2 ft
 
The deepest water will occur along the immediate coast in areas of
onshore winds, where the surge will be accompanied by large and
dangerous waves. Surge-related flooding depends on the relative
timing of the surge and the tidal cycle, and can vary greatly over
short distances. For information specific to your area, please see
products issued by your local National Weather Service forecast
office.
 
For a complete depiction of areas at risk of storm surge inundation,
please see the National Weather Service Peak Storm Surge Graphic,
available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?peakSurge.
 
WIND: Hurricane conditions are expected within the Hurricane Warning
area tonight into early Saturday, with tropical storm conditions
beginning later today. Tropical storm conditions are expected in
the warning areas beginning in the next few hours.
 
RAINFALL:  Through the weekend, Isaias is expected to produce
rainfall amounts of 4 to 8 inches across southern Alabama, the
Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and southwest Georgia. Localized
totals up to 15 inches are possible, particularly within banding
across the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend.
 
Across much of the remainder of the Southeast, rainfall amounts of 2
to 5 inches are expected.  Localized higher amounts up to 10 inches
are possible, especially across the Southern Appalachians and
coastal Carolinas.
 
Flash and urban flooding is likely across the region, with
considerable impacts possible across much of southern Alabama and
the Florida Panhandle into southwestern Georgia. River flooding is
also expected, some of which could be significant.
 
For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with
Isaias, please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall
Graphic available at
hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf and the Flash Flood Risk
graphic at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.
 
TORNADOES: A tornado risk will begin late this afternoon and
continue tonight in coastal areas of Alabama, the Florida Panhandle,
and far southwest Georgia. The tornado threat will continue Saturday
across parts of eastern Alabama, the Florida Panhandle, Georgia, and
the Carolinas.
 
SURF: Swells from Isaias are affecting the western and northern
portions of the Yucatan Peninsula and will spread northward toward
the Gulf Coast later today. These swells are likely to cause
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions. Please consult
products from your local weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents
