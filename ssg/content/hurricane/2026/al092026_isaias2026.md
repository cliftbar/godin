## Official Advisory Discussion
At 400 PM CDT (2100 UTC), the center of Hurricane Isaias was located
near latitude 29.2 North, longitude 87.0 West. Isaias is moving
toward the north near 17 mph (28 km/h), and this general motion is 
expected to continue through Saturday. A north-northeastward to 
northeastward motion is forecast on Sunday. On the forecast track, 
Isaias is expected to make landfall along the coast of the western 
Florida Panhandle this evening. The center is then expected to move 
across Alabama and into the Tennessee Valley Saturday and Sunday.
 
Maximum sustained winds are near 115 mph (185 km/h) with higher
gusts.  Isaias is a category 3 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson
Hurricane Wind Scale.  Little change in strength is expected before 
Isaias makes landfall this evening. Weakening is expected to begin 
tonight after landfall, and Isaias is forecast to become 
post-tropical on Saturday.
 
Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 35 miles (55 km) from the
center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 205 miles
(335 km). A sustained wind of 46 mph (74 km/h) with a gust to 58 
mph (93 km/h) was recently measured at Fair Point Light 2 near Gulf 
Breeze, Florida. A wind gust to 56 mph (90 km/h) was reported at 
the Pensacola Naval Air Station.
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 959 mb (28.32 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Isaias can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header MIATCDAT4 and WMO header WTNT44 KNHC.
 
STORM SURGE: The combination of a dangerous storm surge and the
tide will cause normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by
rising waters moving inland from the shoreline. The water could
reach the following heights above ground somewhere in the indicated
areas if the peak surge occurs at the time of high tide...
 
AL/FL Border to Grayton Beach, FL...6-9 ft
Grayton Beach, FL to Indian Pass, FL...4-7 ft
Indian Pass, FL to Steinhatchee River, FL...4-6 ft
Dauphin Island, AL to AL/FL Border...4-6 ft
Steinhatchee River, FL to Suwannee River, FL...3-5 ft
Mouth of the Mississippi River, LA to Dauphin Island, AL...3-5 ft
Lower Mobile Bay...3-5 ft
Suwannee River, FL to Yankeetown, FL...2-4 ft
Upper Mobile Bay...2-4 ft
 
The deepest water will occur along the immediate coast in areas of
onshore winds, where the surge will be accompanied by large and
dangerous waves. Surge-related flooding depends on the relative
timing of the surge and the tidal cycle, and can vary greatly over
short distances. For information specific to your area, please see
products issued by your local National Weather Service forecast
office.
 
For a complete depiction of areas at risk of storm surge inundation,
please see the National Weather Service Peak Storm Surge Graphic,
available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?peakSurge.
 
WIND: Within the Hurricane Warning area, tropical storm conditions
are now occurring, and hurricane conditions are expected this
evening into early Saturday. Hurricane-force winds are expected to
spread inland as far as 100 miles from the coast into southern
Alabama. Tropical storm conditions are also occurring in the
tropical storm warning areas.
 
RAINFALL:  From Friday through the weekend, Isaias is expected to 
produce rainfall amounts of 4 to 8 inches across southern Alabama, 
the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and southwest Georgia. Localized 
totals up to 15 inches are possible, particularly within banding 
across the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend. 

Across much of the remainder of the Southeast, rainfall amounts of 2 
to 5 inches are expected.  Localized higher amounts up to 10 inches 
are possible, especially across the Southern Appalachians and 
coastal Carolinas.  

Flash, urban, and river flooding is possible across the region, with 
considerable impacts likely across much of southern Alabama and the 
Florida Panhandle into southwestern Georgia. Considerable flash 
flooding and landslides are also possible across the Southern 
Appalachians. 

For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with 
Isaias, please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall 
Graphic available at
hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf and the Flash Flood Risk 
graphic at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.
 
TORNADOES: The risk for tornadoes this afternoon in the Florida 
Panhandle will continue into this evening and tonight across the 
Florida Panhandle, southeast Alabama, and southern Georgia. The 
tornado threat will continue Saturday across parts of eastern 
Alabama, the Florida Panhandle, Georgia, and the Carolinas.
 
SURF: Swells from Isaias are affecting much of the Gulf coasts of
the United States, Mexico, and western Cuba. These swells are
likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
Please consult products from your local weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents
