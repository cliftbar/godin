## Official Advisory Discussion
At 400 AM CDT (0900 UTC), the center of Tropical Storm Isaias was 
located near latitude 22.0 North, longitude 94.1 West. Isaias is 
moving toward the east-northeast near 8 mph (13 km/h). A continued 
east-northeastward motion is expected today, followed by a turn 
toward the northeast and north on Thursday and Friday. On the 
forecast track, Isaias is expected to pass to the north of the 
Yucatan Peninsula on Thursday and approach the U.S. northern Gulf 
Coast on Friday.
 
Maximum sustained winds have increased to near 40 mph (65 km/h) with
higher gusts. Rapid strengthening is forecast over the next day or
two, and Isaias is expected to become a hurricane by Thursday.
 
Tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 45 miles (75 km)
from the center.
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 1004 mb (29.65 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Isaias can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header MIATCDAT4 and WMO header WTNT44 KNHC.
 
RAINFALL: Isaias is expected to produce rainfall amounts of 2 to 4 
inches through Thursday across coastal portions of the Bay of 
Campeche with isolated higher amounts possible in the states of 
Campeche and western Yucatan.

From Friday through the weekend, Isaias is expected to produce 
rainfall amounts of 3 to 6 inches, with localized totals up to 10 
inches, across the northern U.S. Gulf Coast extending from far 
southeastern Louisiana to the Florida Panhandle. Rainfall amounts of 
1 to 3 inches, with localized amounts up to 5 inches, are possible 
across much of the Southeast as far north as the Tennessee Valley 
and the Carolinas. Flash and urban flooding, along with isolated 
river flooding, will be possible.
 
For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with 
Isaias, please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall 
Graphic available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?rainqpf and 
the Flash Flood Risk graphic at 
hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ero.
 
WIND: Gusty winds are possible in portions of the Yucatan Peninsula
on Thursday.
 
SURF: Swells are expected to affect the western and northern
portions of the Yucatan Peninsula beginning on Thursday and spread
northward toward the Gulf Coast late this week. These swells are
likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
Please consult products from your local weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found
at: hurricanes.gov/graphics_at4.shtml?ripCurrents
